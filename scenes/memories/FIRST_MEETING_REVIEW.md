# First Meeting — design review

Reviewed November 11, 2026. The scene represents November 12, 2025: meeting outside the house, followed by the arcade bar. Personal fragment wording belongs to the user; the current headings and notes are placeholders, not established details of the memory.

## Current assessment

The bungalow, large front-yard autumn tree, doorway vines, pale fence, and orange/red groundcover give the house a coherent identity. The open gate and continuous platform support both players. Worn paving and scattered leaves soften the transition into white space. The arcade's loose activity triangle, foliage pockets, suspended lights, and fading floor traces suggest a remembered place without enclosing it in a room.

Pinball, punching bag, and the mechanical band provide distinct small interactions. The two fragments establish the story's main beats. Component recall, white interaction outlines, and restrained painterly materials form a consistent presentation. The scene would benefit more from connected moments and a clear conclusion than from a larger prop inventory.

## Guide implemented and verified

Guidance is now manual: the bottom-right **Find next fragment** circle, or **E**, sends one brushstroke from the player's current position toward the first unfinished fragment. It fades after one pass. Closing a note updates the next destination but never starts another hint. Authored anchors and waypoints keep routes clear of the fence and scenery; the button remains disabled until the active brushstroke finishes.

## Recommended next improvements — not implemented

1. **Share the small moments over LAN.** Current networking carries player poses; fragment state and activity responses are local. Mirror instrument animations/sounds and fragment discovery so each person can notice their partner's actions. Keep note reading independent so one player cannot rush the other through personal text.

2. **Author a quiet closing beat.** `Exit` is a marker and `SignatureInteraction` is empty; there is no memory-completion response. After the arcade note is finished, a short shared glow, a gentle chord, or suspended lights settling into warmth could acknowledge the visit. Offer a return to the timeline without forcing departure or adding a score screen.

3. **Clean up accents and interaction aprons.** Thin foreground cards around console approaches and leave space around the arcade fragment. Also refit `SidewalkLeaves`: its old X=-6.55…6.55 scatter no longer matches the moved sidewalk at roughly X=-1.71…8.29. Taper those accents into the paving instead of leaving an orphaned leaf field. Preserve planting behind and beside the props.

4. **Give the locations a restrained sound identity.** The scene currently has triggered band notes but no environmental soundscape. A soft autumn rustle near the house and a faint mechanical musical texture near the arcade could crossfade along the route. Keep these quiet enough for the fragments to remain contemplative.

5. **Connect each personal note to one visual response.** Once the user has written the text, choose a detail that supports it: perhaps porch/window warmth or a brief instrument response. This can make reading feel embedded in the place. Select the response from the actual memory rather than inventing events or adding generic decoration.
