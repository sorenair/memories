# New Year's Eve — December 31, 2025

Run `scenes/world/new_years_eve_world.tscn` with F6, or recall **New Year's Eve** from the timeline. This is the fifth authored memory, with stable ID `new_years_eve` and one standard **Concert** zone.

## Brief and composition

Confirmed: a New Year's Eve rave/concert, December 31, 2025, starting behind a large crowd with a route to the stage. The focus is finding each other in the music. The venue, artist, actual soundtrack, and personal fragment wording remain unspecified; there is no invented venue, midnight event, or weather.

The single invisible support floor widens from a close rear arrival into two audience banks, leaving the path and scenery in the ivory void without a gray ground cover. A 3.2 m central aisle, middle cross-passage, and open front apron make the crowd traversable for both explorers. The fragment is on the apron, before the stage's continuous ramp. Walking farther leads onto the deck and around the console. The Exit marker on the deck is an authoring anchor, not a completion trigger. The inherited timeline control remains the way home.

Two hundred seeded visitors use the existing crowd MultiMesh/shader motion and chunked hitboxes, with independent poses, height, sway, and bob. Seven moving painted lights, an animated pigment screen, and faint stage-edge haze suggest an expansive concert without building a room around it. The warm-white void stays visible; color is concentrated in muted violet, teal, and gold accents.

The library was searched first: stage/ramp, speakers, rave lights, concert screen, crowd, smoke, paving, finite platform, fragment, guidance, audio, and world/LAN infrastructure are reused. The only new scenery component is `components/scenery/concert/dj/painterly_dj_booth.tscn`: a batched console with two decks, mixer, headphones, and a performer built from existing painted person geometry. Two connected arms scratch independently over rotating platters. Eight small mesh instances animate without rebuilding geometry; hands stay on their own records and above the console. Small LEDs pulse in the shared concert shader.

Camera framing opens as the explorer moves through the crowd, favors the screen near the front, then closes around the DJ on the deck. Fixed yaw and exponential blending work in reverse too. The close arrival matches First Meeting.

Stage hardware shares **Scenery/StageApproach** as its recall anchor: the console, screen, speakers, and rig assemble while approaching the front, rather than requiring the explorer to climb onto the stage first. Crowd and peripheral components retain their own proximity recall.

The soundtrack is an original 16-second, 120 BPM electronic placeholder generated by `tools/generate_rave_audio.py`. The zone reuses the quiet synthetic murmur bed. Music and Ambience mix independently through the standard memory buses, alongside fragment SFX. Visuals are configured to 120 BPM but are decorative, not audio-reactive or phase-locked. Replace the WAV or assign another Music Stream when the actual soundtrack is chosen.

The shared world supplies both avatars, partner interpolation, safe rear spawns, controls, guidance, and fragment UI. Scenic animation and audio remain local to each device, as in the other memories.

## Verify and preview

```sh
godot --headless --path . --fixed-fps 60 --script tests/new_years_eve.gd
godot --headless --path . --script tests/memory_lane_smoke.gd
godot --path . --fixed-fps 60 --resolution 1600x738 --script tests/new_years_eve.gd -- --capture
```

The scene check walks the real explorer through the central aisle and cross-passage, activates the fragment, climbs the ramp, circles beside the DJ, and returns to arrival. It checks supported audience placement, safe paired spawns, camera changes, simultaneous music/ambience, requested guidance, dated timeline recall, and character choice. A full-strength DJ motion sweep checks connected arms, hands over their own records, and vertex clearance above the console and platters. Rendered previews are saved as `docs/previews/new-years-eve-{arrival,crowd,stage,dj,overview}.png`. Physical iOS devices are not covered by these checks.

For a two-process LAN smoke check, run the same script with `-- host` and `-- guest` (without `--fixed-fps`). Both clients must pair, show the other chosen avatar, replicate walking down the rear aisle, and stay grounded. The local headless host/guest check passes; Godot's dummy renderer emits its existing null-material messages during avatar recall. This does not validate physical network or iOS rendering.
