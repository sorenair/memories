# Memory Lane

Fragment notes use the same warm-white, charcoal, and serif language as the menus. `fragments/fragment_interaction.gd` presents a quiet uppercase heading over centered prose, with an arrow to continue and an X to close, each inside a generous invisible touch target. The panel has softly rounded corners. Short notes size to their content; longer notes scroll within a bounded reading area. Opening, page changes, and dismissal fade softly. The panel clears the joystick and guidance circle in landscape and sits above them in portrait. Existing fragment queues and per-fragment completion callbacks are preserved. Verify presentation behavior with `tests/fragment_note_ui.gd` and gameplay with `tests/fragments.gd`.

The bottom-right circle with a small diamond is **Find next fragment**, opposite the joystick. Tap, click, or press **E** to send one white painterly brushstroke from the player's current position toward the first unfinished fragment in the memory's authored order. The circle stays faint and disabled while a brushstroke travels and fades at its destination, then becomes available again. Guidance never starts automatically on entry or after notes. The circle is faint and disabled when no unfinished fragments remain, and open notes or arrival/return movement locks block requests. Fragment order is set by `MemoryScene.guidance_fragments`, with Zones child order when empty (scene-order discovery for legacy memories).

Tap or click an in-range component directly to interact. The shared controller picks visible component geometry and gates range, occlusion, consumed targets, arrival/return, and open fragment notes. See [the interaction contract](../scripts/interaction/README.md) to add targets or observe request/success signals, and [the brushstroke guide](../components/guidance/brushstroke/README.md) to author safe guidance routes.

The in-game top-right dot-and-line glyph samples the scene behind its strokes and switches each pixel to contrasting black or white. Only the icon is drawn, with no halo or background plate; thresholded inverted grayscale avoids disappearing against mid-gray. It returns to the timeline through a warm-white fade, preserving the selected memory. Its 112 × 112 invisible target supports touch, mouse, and keyboard focus; Escape also returns. Drags and cancelled touches do not activate it, and it stays disabled during arrival. The reusable button lives in `in_game/timeline_button.tscn`. The FPS readout is offset to its left to keep the touch target clear. Validate navigation with `godot --headless --path . --script tests/timeline_return_smoke.gd`.

In-game movement uses one fixed bottom-left joystick, defined in `res://scripts/exploration_input.gd`. Its fine outline and center circle are translucent at rest, smoothly become opaque when held, and fade back while recentering on release. A small deadzone prevents thumb jitter; movement strength beyond it is analog. Only touches beginning on or just outside the ring activate movement, and additional fingers cannot steal the stick. Release, cancellation, focus loss, and resizing clear movement. Keyboard/controller input remains available; mouse dragging supports desktop preview. Radius, thumb size, edge padding, and deadzone are editable on ExplorationInput. Run `godot --headless --path . --script tests/touch_joystick_smoke.gd` for focused control checks.

Run the project (F5) to open `main_menu/main_menu.tscn`: a warm-white EVERMORE title screen with an infinity symbol made from two outlined circles below. The circles gently breathe inward and brighten to invite tapping while the touch target stays still. On activation, the title and corner controls fade as the circles move upward together, merge at the screen center, then fill dark to become the timeline selection point. The timeline grows outward only after the merge and fill. `main_menu/timeline_transition.gd` carries the same drawing across the scene swap and hands it to `memory_lane/memory_lane.tscn` without a blank frame or duplicate point. Character selection lives only on the main menu: custom male/female SVG icons in the bottom-right corner choose Soren/Vivian, with the unselected icon at 18% opacity. The chosen avatar persists through the timeline and into solo/LAN play. The timeline’s upper-left back arrow or Escape returns to the main menu to change character. Open either scene directly with F6 for iteration. The close-scale menu is text-only for now, with the timeline vertically centered, the memory title above, the selected date below, and a larger circular counterclockwise Recall arrow underneath. Its enlarged invisible touch area and light stroke match the other controls; a short tangent stem keeps both arrowhead wings clear of the curved tail. Whole-story zoom and diorama previews are deferred.

- Drag horizontally with a finger or mouse to move the uniformly spaced memory marks beneath the fixed selection point; release to snap.
- Tap a nearby mark or date to select it. Left/right arrow keys also select memories.
- Tap the **Recall loop**, click it, or press Enter to fade into the selected memory’s playable world. First Meeting loads `res://scenes/world/first_meeting_world.tscn`; Football in the snow loads `res://scenes/world/snowy_stadium_world.tscn`; Minneapolis Institute of Art loads `res://scenes/world/art_museum_world.tscn`; Seasonal Demo loads `res://scenes/world/memory_world.tscn`.

The four entries live in `MEMORIES` in `memory_lane.gd`: First Meeting (NOV 12, 2025), selected initially, Football in the snow (labeled COLLEGE FOOTBALL until its date is supplied), Minneapolis Institute of Art (DEC 4, 2025), and Seasonal Demo (labeled DEMO rather than given an invented date). Each entry specifies its scene path. The warm-white screen uses system serif fonts (Georgia, Noto Serif, DejaVu Serif, then Godot's fallback), with no preview images. Layout adapts to the Control's available size; the project retains its existing landscape viewport and stretch settings. Touch is handled directly, including when mouse emulation is disabled. Actual iOS hardware has not been tested.

Opening the timeline directly runs a 1.8-second entrance that reveals the central point, unfolds the thread outward with soft leading edges, settles neighboring marks and dates, then fades in the title and Recall. Entering from the title screen uses a one-second shared circle merge and fill, followed by the timeline reveal. Navigation unlocks when the timeline takes ownership of the point; touch, mouse click, or keyboard navigation then finishes any remaining entrance immediately. Reopening the menu within the same application session uses a shorter 0.4-second fade. Animation timings are defined in `memory_lane.gd`; resizing preserves the current animation stage.

Recall fades the UI to warm white over 0.45 seconds. The destination consumes a one-shot arrival request, places the player at the memory entry plus its supported recalled offset, and frames the camera before the first frame. First Meeting uses its sidewalk Entry; the demo retains its 10 m offset. The same veil bridges the scene swap, then uncovers the world over 0.45 seconds. `memory_arrival/memory_arrival.gd` uses the same explorer recall as remote players: an upward white-edge scan over 1.25 seconds with a fading contact shadow. The body and visual remain grounded throughout. Movement and touch controls pause until the scan finishes, then authored materials and normal movement return.

Returning to the timeline reverses that same scan over 0.95 seconds before the 0.35-second paper fade. Movement pauses immediately; collision and LAN memory presence are cleared as the departure starts so observers see the matching reverse effect. The local effect retains the player until the scene swap. Navigation failure restores materials, collision, input, audio and memory presence. The shared timings live in `scripts/network/remote_presence.gd`.

Tune `recalled_spawn_offset` on the MemoryWorld node. Running the world directly with F6 retains its normal entry beside the demo scenery and skips the Recall arrival sequence.

Memory worlds share `memory_world_base.tscn` for solo and LAN player setup. The connection persists across the main menu, timeline, and memories; each player chooses their own memory. Only a matching stable `memory_id` enables partner playback and local player contact. Protocol version 4 exchanges per-player presence and visit IDs rather than forcing the host's catalog choice, so both devices need this updated build.

The character and utility icons are editable SVGs under `main_menu/icons/`; the two-circle infinity and circular Recall are animated vector drawings in `main_menu/memory_action_button.gd`. No raster assets or system symbol fonts are required. Their invisible touch targets support mouse, keyboard focus, and native touch without mouse emulation. Dragged and cancelled touches do not activate them. `tests/main_menu.gd` verifies character selection, opacity, Enter navigation, the full timeline entrance, portrait layout, and returning to the title screen.

Host/Join controls live only in `main_menu/lan_controls.gd`, at the bottom left opposite character selection. The first main-menu opening automatically starts hosting. Host and Join share a single address field and fixed icon slots, so switching modes changes neither their position nor size. The host field is read-only with no underline or arrow; Join edits the same field and fades in its submit arrow. The empty arrow slot is retained in Host to keep the row steady. Host/Join labels use only active/pressed and inactive colors, with no hover brightening or focus underline. Two faint separated rings interlock and darken when connected, with a quiet pulse while joining. Routine waiting/connected text and the IP prefix are omitted; actionable errors still appear above the row. Active Host/Join buttons also allow disconnecting. Reopening the menu preserves the current connection or an explicit disconnect instead of starting hosting again. Joining has a timeout and can be cancelled by tapping Join again, while Enter and Recall remain available throughout. Connection details sit above the bottom controls in portrait, leaving their touch targets separate.
