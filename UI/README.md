# Memory Lane

The top-left HUD shows one ring for each fragment in the current memory, aligned with the timeline button. A ring fills when a fragment is first collected, and the tracker resets on a new visit. Like the timeline button, the glyphs adapt to the scene behind them.

Fragment notes use the same warm-white, charcoal, and serif language as the menus. `fragments/fragment_interaction.gd` presents a quiet uppercase heading over centered prose, with an arrow to continue and an X to close, each inside a generous invisible touch target. The panel has softly rounded corners. Short notes size to their content; longer notes scroll within a bounded reading area. Opening, page changes, and dismissal fade softly. The panel clears the joystick and guidance circle in landscape and sits above them in portrait. Existing fragment queues and per-fragment completion callbacks are preserved. Verify presentation behavior with `tests/fragment_note_ui.gd` and gameplay with `tests/fragments.gd`.

The bottom-right circle with a small diamond is **Find next fragment**, opposite the joystick. Tap, click, or press **E** to send one white painterly brushstroke from the player's current position toward the first unfinished fragment in the memory's authored order. The circle stays faint and disabled while a brushstroke travels and fades at its destination, then becomes available again. Guidance never starts automatically on entry or after notes. The circle is faint and disabled when no unfinished fragments remain, and open notes or arrival/return movement locks block requests. Fragment order is set by `MemoryScene.guidance_fragments`, with Zones child order when empty (scene-order discovery for legacy memories).

Tap or click an in-range component directly to interact. The shared controller picks visible component geometry and gates range, occlusion, consumed targets, arrival/return, and open fragment notes. See [the interaction contract](../scripts/interaction/README.md) to add targets or observe request/success signals, and [the brushstroke guide](../components/guidance/brushstroke/README.md) to author safe guidance routes.

The in-game top-right dot-and-line glyph samples the scene behind its strokes and switches each pixel to contrasting black or white. Only the icon is drawn, with no halo or background plate; thresholded inverted grayscale avoids disappearing against mid-gray. It returns to the timeline through a warm-white fade, preserving the selected memory. Its 128 × 128 invisible target supports touch, mouse, and keyboard focus; Escape also returns. Drags and cancelled touches do not activate it, and it stays disabled during arrival. The reusable button lives in `in_game/timeline_button.tscn`. The FPS readout is offset to its left to keep the touch target clear. Validate navigation with `godot --headless --path . --script tests/timeline_return_smoke.gd`.

In-game movement uses one floating joystick with a bottom-left resting position, defined in `res://scripts/exploration_input.gd`. Its fine outline and center circle are translucent at rest, smoothly become opaque when held, and fade back while recentering on release. A small deadzone prevents thumb jitter; movement strength beyond it is analog. The stick appears where a touch starts in the lower-left quarter and glides home upon release. Drag to move or tap an in-range object to interact. Additional fingers cannot steal the stick. Release, cancellation, focus loss, and resizing clear movement. Keyboard/controller input remains available; mouse dragging supports desktop preview. Radius, thumb size, edge padding, and deadzone are editable on ExplorationInput. Run `godot --headless --path . --script tests/touch_joystick_smoke.gd` for focused control checks.

The close-scale menu is text-only for now, with the timeline vertically centered, the memory title above, the selected date below, and a larger circular counterclockwise Recall arrow underneath. Its enlarged invisible touch area and light stroke match the other controls; its opening stays centered at twelve o'clock, and a short tangent stem keeps both arrowhead wings clear of the curved tail. A quiet highlight traces the loop during idle moments, the ring responds gently to hover or touch, and Recall triggers a faster trace and inward breath during the existing paper fade. The Whole story scale fits the actual catalog across the available width, with adaptive month/year labels and finer memory ticks. Diorama previews remain deferred.

Story layout lives in `memory_lane/story_layout.gd`. It spreads every catalog size between responsive side margins rather than using a fixed overview spacing. Month groups occupy space according to their actual memories; labels are culled when they would overlap, and long histories emphasize years. Each year label anchors to its first memory; tapping that label selects the first memory of the year. All overview memory ticks have the same height and stroke width. Closely spaced ticks are combined visually into narrow density marks while every entry remains selectable. Add `period: "YYYY-MM"` to dated catalog entries; entries without calendar dates retain their `period_label` (for example Snow or Demo). No dates are inferred for them. Memory and Whole story are two settled scales. A pinch or trackpad gesture chooses the other scale after a small intentional movement; it does not directly scrub the zoom amount. Each transfer takes about 1.1 seconds with smooth acceleration and deceleration, perceptually even spacing changes, and labels that travel with their marks. Continuing the same gesture or repeating the same request does not speed up or restart the transition. Interrupted zooms retain their motion and preserve the thread position when a new memory is tapped. The selected mark moves continuously between its fitted story position and the fixed close-view center; returning from gameplay opens the close view around the remembered selection. Native touch gestures share one input coordinator so a second finger cancels a pending Recall tap.

Opening the timeline directly runs a 1.8-second entrance that reveals the central point, unfolds the thread outward with soft leading edges, settles neighboring marks and dates, then fades in the title and Recall. Entering from the title screen uses a one-second shared circle merge and fill, followed by the timeline reveal. Navigation unlocks when the timeline takes ownership of the point; touch, mouse click, or keyboard navigation then finishes any remaining entrance immediately. Reopening the menu within the same application session uses a shorter 0.4-second fade. Animation timings are defined in `memory_lane.gd`; resizing preserves the current animation stage.

Recall fades the UI to warm white over 0.45 seconds. The destination consumes a one-shot arrival request, places the player at the memory entry plus its supported recalled offset, and frames the camera before the first frame. First Meeting uses its sidewalk Entry; the demo retains its 10 m offset. The same veil bridges the scene swap, then uncovers the world over 0.45 seconds. `memory_arrival/memory_arrival.gd` uses the same explorer recall as remote players: an upward white-edge scan over 1.25 seconds with a fading contact shadow. The body and visual remain grounded throughout. Movement and touch controls pause until the scan finishes, then authored materials and normal movement return.

Returning to the timeline reverses that same scan over 0.95 seconds before the 0.35-second paper fade. Movement pauses immediately; collision and LAN memory presence are cleared as the departure starts so observers see the matching reverse effect. The local effect retains the player until the scene swap. Navigation failure restores materials, collision, input, audio and memory presence. The shared timings live in `scripts/network/remote_presence.gd`.

Memory worlds share `memory_world_base.tscn` for solo and LAN player setup. The connection persists across the main menu, timeline, and memories; each player chooses their own memory. Only a matching stable `memory_id` enables partner playback and local player contact. Protocol version 4 exchanges per-player presence and visit IDs rather than forcing the host's catalog choice, so both devices need this updated build.

The character and utility icons are editable SVGs under `main_menu/icons/`; the two-circle infinity and circular Recall are animated vector drawings in `main_menu/memory_action_button.gd`. No raster assets or system symbol fonts are required. Their invisible touch targets support mouse, keyboard focus, and native touch without mouse emulation. Dragged and cancelled touches do not activate them. `tests/main_menu.gd` verifies character selection, opacity, Enter navigation, the full timeline entrance, portrait layout, and returning to the title screen.

Host/Join controls live only in `main_menu/lan_controls.gd`, at the bottom left opposite character selection. The first main-menu opening automatically starts hosting. Host and Join share a single address field and fixed icon slots, so switching modes changes neither their position nor size. The host field is read-only with no underline or arrow; Join edits the same field and fades in its submit arrow. The empty arrow slot is retained in Host to keep the row steady. Host/Join labels use only active/pressed and inactive colors, with no hover brightening or focus underline. A minimal padlock closes when a partner is connected and opens when disconnected. Its body and keyhole stay fixed; only the hinged shackle moves through a soft 0.45-second transition. While joining, only the shackle has a quiet opacity pulse. Validate the fixed-body animation with `tests/connection_indicator.gd`. Routine waiting/connected text and the IP prefix are omitted; actionable errors still appear above the row. Active Host/Join buttons also allow disconnecting. Reopening the menu preserves the current connection or an explicit disconnect instead of starting hosting again. Joining has a timeout and can be cancelled by tapping Join again, while Enter and Recall remain available throughout. Connection details sit above the bottom controls in portrait, leaving their touch targets separate.
